# Legal-first, cover-only, adaptation-licensed catalog

We only publish **Covers** (our own re-created instrumental + a sung English
vocal) of **Clearable Songs** — songs that are public domain, Creative Commons,
or whose publisher/artist has personally granted the **Adaptation Right**. We
never use the original **Master**, and we never publish a translated version of
a song we have not cleared.

## Why

Translating lyrics is an *adaptation* of the musical work — a right the
publisher controls per song. It is **not** covered by the blanket mechanical
licences that make ordinary same-language covers safe on YouTube, and it is
separate again from the Master (sound-recording) copyright. So a translated
cover of a famous song is an unlicensed derivative no matter how original our
instrumental is, and Content ID / strikes can delete the channel.

## Considered options

- **Publish broadly, rely on Content ID / accept takedowns** — fastest, highest
  volume, but translated lyrics are unlicensed derivatives; a few strikes end
  the channel. Rejected.
- **Clear every song before publishing (including hits)** — safest, but slow and
  expensive; kills launch momentum. Deferred, not the launch model.
- **Legal-first catalog (chosen)** — launch only on Clearable Songs, buy
  adaptation licences for specific hits later once there's traction and revenue.

## Consequences

- The launch catalog is **indie / CC / personally-permissioned** songs, not the
  famous chart hits everyone knows. We trade reach for a channel nobody can pull
  down. Channel identity is "discovering and translating" these songs.
- Every video must re-create its instrumental — ideally from the artist's own
  stems, negotiated together with the Adaptation Right in one conversation.
