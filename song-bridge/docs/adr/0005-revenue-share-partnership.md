# Revenue-share partnership as the standard artist deal

The default way we acquire a Clearable Song is a **Revenue-Share Partnership**
with a **Partner Artist**: we split the video's YouTube revenue and prominently
credit + link their original; in return they grant the Adaptation Right and hand
over the instrumental stems.

## Why

Legal-first (ADR-0001) needs a *repeatable yes* from indie artists, and a
signature singer (ADR-0004) caps throughput — so the pitch must be attractive
enough to convert reliably. Revenue-share aligns incentives: the artist gains a
new English-speaking audience and passive income for work already done, and is
motivated to re-share the video, which doubles as free marketing. It also gets
the stems and adaptation right in a single conversation, with no upfront cash.

## Considered options

- **Flat per-song licence fee** — cleaner ownership, but cash-intensive upfront
  and slower to scale; weaker incentive for the artist to promote. Rejected as
  the default (still usable case-by-case).
- **Exposure-only (credit + backlink, no money)** — cheapest, but the weakest
  incentive and can read as exploitative, especially before the channel has real
  reach. Rejected.
- **Revenue-share partnership (chosen)** — incentive-aligned, no upfront cash,
  self-marketing.

## Consequences

- Revenue is shared from day one, so per-video margin is lower — accepted in
  exchange for supply, goodwill, and marketing.
- Needs a simple, repeatable partnership agreement (revenue split %, credit
  terms, stem hand-off, adaptation grant) as a reusable template.
