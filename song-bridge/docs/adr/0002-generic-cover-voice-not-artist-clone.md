# Generic Cover Voice, not an artist voice-clone

The sung English vocal uses a generic or licensed **Cover Voice** that delivers
a faithful performance. We deliberately do **not** clone the Source Song
artist's specific vocal identity, even though "sounds exactly like the original
singer" would be a stronger hook.

## Why

Cloning a named artist's voice is increasingly illegal and against platform
policy — Tennessee's ELVIS Act, Denmark's likeness law, and YouTube's own
AI-likeness rules all target voice/likeness impersonation, on top of any
right-of-publicity claim. A generic faithful cover sidesteps that entire class
of risk and composes cleanly with the legal-first catalog (ADR-0001): generic
voice + cleared song = a video with no impersonation and no master use.

## Consequences

- We lose the "it really sounds like them" wow factor; the draw is the *song and
  the singable English adaptation*, not mimicry of a famous voice.
- Voice sourcing (human singer vs. licensed synthetic voice) stays an open
  production choice — the only hard constraint is "not an identity-clone."
